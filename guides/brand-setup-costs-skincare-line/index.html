Brand Setup Costs Founders Leave Out

A launch budget built only from a manufacturing quote is incomplete. This guide covers the setup work that sits outside manufacturing, so it can be budgeted alongside production instead of discovered afterwards.

Updated 28 October 2026 • General educational information, not a quote. Every manufacturer prices differently — treat the ranges below as starting points for your own quotes.

The bucket outside manufacturing

Manufacturing quotes arrive as a single document, with the formula, the fill, the packaging, the labels, and the freight gathered into a set of lines that can be totalled and compared. Setup work does not arrive that way.

The published band for brand setup in the cost guide is $300–$2,500 one-off (estimate based on general industry patterns — not a quote). The band is a one-off band, which means it describes setup work carried out for a launch, rather than a recurring charge or a per-unit charge.

For the full set of launch buckets, see brand and business setup costs in the skincare line cost guide. Brand setup sits as the final bucket in the cost guide worksheet, after formula, fill, packaging, labels, testing, samples, and freight, and its position at the end of the worksheet is part of why it is overlooked.

Give setup its own list. A separate setup list, with business registration, trademark filing, barcode registration, photography, and the sales channel each named, turns scattered charges into a visible bucket that can be budgeted before launch.

The timing of setup work matters as much as its cost. Some setup items need to be started early because they take time, depend on a decision that affects other work, or must be in place before a later step can proceed.

Business registration and filings

Business registration is the formal step of establishing the business that will own the brand, hold the accounts, and stand behind the launch. The form that registration takes depends on the founder's circumstances and on where the business will operate, and founders commonly discuss the options with a qualified adviser before choosing.

Filings beyond the initial registration can also belong in this part of the setup list, depending on the founder's circumstances. Those filings vary, and they should be identified through the same conversation with a qualified adviser that informs the registration choice, rather than guessed at from general descriptions.

Record-keeping is the thread that runs through the registration item and through setup work generally. A launch file that holds the registration details, the filing record, and the correspondence for each setup item gives the founder a single place to check what has been done, what is outstanding, and what each item involved.

Trademark filing

Trademark filing is the process of seeking protection for a brand name or mark, and it is one of the setup items founders most often postpone. Postponement is understandable, because filing involves a decision about the name, a check on whether the name is available for the intended use, and a process that takes time and involves professional input for many founders. The cost guide lists trademark filing as part of the brand setup bucket, without stating a separate figure for it, and the setup band of $300–$2,500 one-off (estimate based on general industry patterns — not a quote) is the published figure that covers the bucket as a whole.

The name decision itself deserves unhurried attention, because the name will appear on labels, cartons, listings, and sales material, and a name that changes late creates revision work across all of them. A founder should check the name carefully before artwork is finalised, before the sales channel is built around it, and before photography captions and listing copy are written.

Filing is not the only way founders protect a name in the early stages, and this guide does not recommend a particular course. Some founders file early, some file after the first launch has shown that the name and the range have a future, and some rely on other forms of protection while the brand is established.

Where professional input is involved, the founder should ask what the input covers, what it does not cover, and what further steps would be needed after the initial filing. Trademark work can involve a search, an application, responses to questions raised during examination, and renewal considerations, and each stage can involve its own timing and charges.

Barcode registration

Barcode registration is the step that gives each product a code that can be scanned at a till, in a warehouse, and through a retail system. Barcodes matter most when products will be sold through retailers or through systems that rely on scanning, and they matter less for a launch sold directly through a founder's own channel, where scanning may not be involved. The cost guide lists barcode registration as part of the brand setup bucket, without a separate published figure, and the setup band of $300–$2,500 one-off (estimate based on general industry patterns — not a quote) covers the bucket as a whole.

The number of codes needed is tied to the number of products and variants, because each distinct product and variant that will be scanned needs its own code. A launch with a small set of products, each in a single variant, needs a small number of codes, while a launch with several variants needs a code for each variant that will be sold separately.

Barcode artwork and label layout interact as well, because the barcode must be placed on the label or carton at a size and in a position where it can be scanned reliably. The label design should leave a clear space for the barcode, with enough quiet space around it for scanning, and the barcode should be checked against the actual container and carton before printing.

Photography and the sales channel

Product photography is the setup item founders mention most often when they describe a launch surprise, and it is discussed in depth in the companion guide on photography budgeting. For the setup budget, the point to record is that photography sits inside the brand setup bucket, that it is often the largest item within that bucket, and that it needs its own figure, its own scheduling, and its own brief, rather than being treated as a minor addition to the manufacturing lines.

The sales channel is the route through which customers will buy the product, whether that is a founder's own online store, a marketplace listing, a wholesale arrangement, or a combination of routes. Setting up a channel involves building the storefront or listing, writing the product descriptions, preparing the images and the pricing, and arranging the way orders, payments, and fulfilment will be handled.

The channel decision interacts with the rest of the setup list in several ways. The channel affects the barcode need, because a retail channel needs scanning while a direct channel may not.

For founders shaping a first range for a particular buyer group, the segment guides offer a useful companion view of launch shape and starter lineups, without covering setup costs. See, for example, launching an indie skincare brand. The segment view helps a founder think about which products to launch and for whom, while this guide helps the founder budget the setup work that any launch shape will need. Used together, they keep the commercial shape and the setup budget in the same conversation, which is where both belong.

Building a setup budget that does not leak

A setup budget leaks when items are recorded in different places, in different formats, at different times, and never gathered into a single view. The remedy is a single setup list, kept in the same place as the manufacturing worksheet, with each item named, a real figure sought for each one, and the timing noted.

  • Name every setup item. Business registration, trademark filing, barcode registration, photography, and the sales channel should each appear by name, with any further filings or setup items added as they are identified.
  • Seek a real figure for each item. Record the figure from the provider or adviser involved, rather than carrying the setup band as a substitute for individual figures.
  • Record the timing for each item. Note when each item must start, what it unblocks, and what would be delayed if it slipped, so the sequence is visible alongside the manufacturing timeline.
  • Revisit the list when decisions change. A new variant, a new channel, or a name change should prompt a review of every setup line it touches, from barcodes to photography to artwork.
  • Keep the launch file together. Hold the registration record, the filing record, the barcode list, the photography brief and images, and the channel details in a single launch file that can be consulted and handed over.
  • Compare the total with the band. Use the published band of $300–$2,500 one-off (estimate based on general industry patterns — not a quote) as a reasonableness check on the gathered total, not as a replacement for it.

The practical conclusion is to treat brand setup as a bucket with the same standing as any manufacturing bucket. It has its own items, its own providers, its own timing, and its own total, and it deserves the same careful listing and the same real figures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Brand setup costs
What setup costs sit outside manufacturing?

Business registration, trademark filing, barcode registration, product photography, and setting up a sales channel all sit outside manufacturing, yet all of them are launch costs that need a place in the first budget.

Why do founders leave setup costs out of first budgets?

Setup costs are easy to leave out because they arrive through different providers and at different times, while manufacturing quotes arrive as one document, so the setup lines never appear together until they are listed deliberately.
