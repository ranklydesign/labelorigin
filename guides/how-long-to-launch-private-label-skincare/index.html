How long does it take to launch a private-label line?

The timeline question has two honest answers, and they are months apart. A stock-formula launch can be on your shelf in under two months. A custom-formulated line can take the better part of a year. The difference is not manufacturing speed — it is whether the formula already exists.

Updated 6 November 2026 • General educational information, not a quote. Every manufacturer works to its own schedule — treat the ranges below as starting points for your own quotes.

How long does it take to launch a private label skincare line?

A stock-formula private-label launch typically ships in 6 to 8 weeks from quote approval, deposit, and finished artwork, according to Trilogy Laboratories. Custom formulation takes longer — 3 to 9 months from concept to first production run. The gap is development and testing, not manufacturing: production itself usually takes 4 to 8 weeks either way.

Those two numbers frame every launch decision. If you need product on the shelf for a season, a holiday, or a new treatment menu, the stock-formula path is the only one that fits. If the formula itself is the point — a signature scent, a proprietary active blend, a texture no stock formula matches — the custom path is the price of that difference, and it should be budgeted in months, not weeks. This guide walks both timelines stage by stage, names what delays them, and shows how to hit the fast end of either one.

The clock starts at approval, not at first contact. Every published timeline runs from quote approval, paid deposit, and finalized artwork. The weeks spent choosing products, revising labels, and waiting on a decision are yours — they do not appear in any manufacturer's timeline.

The two timelines

Manufacturers publish timelines in different ways, but the figures converge. For stock-formula private label, Trilogy Laboratories states 6 to 8 weeks from quote approval, deposit, and finalized artwork to shipment; Skincraftlab states 45 to 60 days from brief to shipped goods for a standard project; NextBeautyLabs publishes a 60-day roadmap for a stock-formula launch done without cutting corners. Three different manufacturers, three ways of counting — the same two months.

For custom formulation, Trilogy puts development at 3 to 9 months from concept to first production run, with production taking a further 6 to 8 weeks after development. Rhine Cosmetics gives a longer range for fully custom work: 9 to 18 months including research and development, with 12 weeks of stability testing alone. Seoul Beauty Labs, working in K-beauty, quotes 14 to 18 weeks concept to shelf for its managed process. The spread is wide because custom work varies — a small tweak to a base formula is months; a formula built from scratch with novel actives is a year. Ask any manufacturer which end of the range your brief falls in before you commit to a launch date.

Stage by stage: a stock-formula launch

A 6-to-8-week launch is not one long wait. It is a sequence of short stages, and each one has its own clock.

Days 1–14: brief and product selection

You send the brief — product types, units per product, packaging preferences, target retail price, timeline — and the manufacturer answers with formula options, minimums, and indicative pricing. Skincraftlab answers a written brief with formula options and pricing within 24 hours. The manufacturer quote brief guide lists exactly what to send so the answer comes back complete; a vague brief costs days in follow-up questions. This is also when you choose the products themselves: pick from the manufacturer's library, order samples, and narrow to the three to five products that fit your treatment menu. The brief scorer rates your brief before you send it.

Days 7–21: samples

Standard formulation samples ship in 3 to 7 days from brief approval (Skincraftlab). You test texture, scent, and performance — on skin, in the treatment room, not just on the back of a hand. Custom-packaged branded samples take longer and are quoted per project. Budget for more than one sample round: the first round almost never ships unchanged, and each round adds its own shipping time. The sample rounds guide covers what to budget for evaluation rounds.

Days 14–30: branding and packaging

While samples are in transit, branding and packaging run in parallel: label design, regulatory text, and pack selection. NextBeautyLabs puts branding and packaging in days 15 to 30 of its 60-day roadmap. Stock packaging with custom labels is the fast choice — custom packaging can add 8 to 12 weeks on its own, which is longer than the rest of the launch combined. If the launch date matters, choose stock packaging for the first run and revisit custom packs for the second.

Days 30–45: manufacturing

Once the formula and packaging are approved, bulk production takes about 20 days for catalogue items (Skincraftlab) or 4 to 8 weeks door to door (ALFA MFG). The difference is scope: the shorter figure is production alone; the longer one includes filling, labeling, quality checks, and release. Stability and compatibility testing runs in parallel where the manufacturer does it in-house, so results accumulate while you check the samples.

Days 45–60: freight and launch preparation

Freight, customs clearance, and artwork revisions should be buffered by two weeks (Skincraftlab). NextBeautyLabs puts fulfillment and launch preparation in days 45 to 60: the shipment arrives, you check it against the approved sample, and the shelf, the treatment-room aftercare forms, and the staff training are ready before the boxes are opened. A launch that arrives with no shelf plan is a launch that sits in the stockroom.

The custom-formulation timeline

Custom formulation adds three stages the stock path skips: development, iteration, and stability testing. Development is a chemist's work — building a formula to your specification, sourcing raw materials, and running trial batches. Iteration is the sample rounds: each round is a new batch, a new shipment, and a new evaluation, and most projects need more than one. Stability testing is the long pole: 12 weeks is the figure Rhine Cosmetics cites, and it cannot be shortened by spending more, because the test is time itself — the product has to sit, under controlled conditions, and prove it does not separate, change colour, or lose efficacy.

That is why the custom range is so wide. A straightforward product — a small change to a known base, one active, standard packaging — can complete development in three months. A complex blend with several actives, a novel texture, or extensive stability protocols runs to nine months or beyond, and production adds a further 6 to 8 weeks after development (Trilogy). If you are weighing the two paths, the private label vs. custom formulation guide compares them on speed, cost, control, and minimums with a decision table.

What delays a launch

  • Custom packaging. Tooling, print, and filling trials add steps — NextBeautyLabs puts the cost at 8 to 12 weeks. Stock packaging with custom labels is the single biggest time saving available.
  • Artwork revisions. Every label revision restarts the artwork clock. Skincraftlab explicitly buffers two weeks for artwork revisions and freight; treat that buffer as part of the plan, not slack to spend.
  • Sample rounds. Each round is a batch, a shipment, and an evaluation. Two rounds are normal on custom formulas; three is not unusual. Decide who approves samples before the first round ships, or the approval itself becomes the delay.
  • Testing failures. A stability or preservative test that fails sends the formula back to development. This is the delay you cannot schedule around — it is also the reason testing exists, and skipping it is not a shortcut.
  • Freight and customs. International freight, customs clearance, and port delays sit outside the manufacturer's control. The two-week buffer Skincraftlab recommends covers the normal case, not the worst one.
  • Decision lag. The most common delay is also the least technical: a brief that waits a week for a decision, a sample that waits a week for feedback, artwork that waits for a sign-off. One decision-maker, with authority to approve, is worth more than any fast manufacturer.

How to hit the fast end

The 60-day launches in NextBeautyLabs' roadmap are not lucky — they are disciplined. The pattern is the same in every fast launch: stock or semi-custom formulas, stock packaging with custom labels, products chosen before the brief is sent, one person authorized to approve samples and artwork, and a launch date set backwards from the shelf, not forwards from the first email. Cut complexity, not corners: every custom element you add — a custom bottle, a custom scent, a novel active — adds its own timeline, and the timelines stack.

Use the launch timeline planner to map your own dates backwards from the launch day: it shows which stages run in parallel and where the slack is. And keep the cost guide open beside it — speed has a price, and the fastest path is usually the stock-formula one, which is also the cheapest. The two decisions, path and date, are the same decision.

Reorders are faster — plan for them

Reorders of existing private-label products typically ship within 3 to 5 weeks (Trilogy), roughly half the first-launch timeline, because sampling, artwork, and testing are already done. That is why the reorder minimums guide says to budget for the second run before the first sells out: the reorder clock is short, but it still exists, and a stockout costs more in lost sales than the reorder does. Order the second run when the first is half sold, not when it is gone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Private label launch timeline
How long does a private label skincare launch take?

A stock-formula private-label launch typically ships in 6 to 8 weeks from quote approval, deposit, and finished artwork (Trilogy Laboratories, checked 31 October 2026). Skincraftlab gives a similar figure: a standard project runs 45 to 60 days from brief to shipped goods. Custom formulation is a different timeline — 3 to 9 months from concept to first production run.

What is the fastest way to launch a skincare line?

Stock formulas with stock packaging and custom labels. NextBeautyLabs publishes a 60-day roadmap on exactly that basis: strategy and product selection in the first two weeks, branding and packaging in weeks three and four, manufacturing in weeks five to seven, and fulfillment in the final two weeks. Custom packaging alone can add 8 to 12 weeks.

How long do samples take?

Standard formulation samples ship in 3 to 7 days from brief approval (Skincraftlab, checked 31 October 2026). Custom-packaged branded samples take longer and are quoted per project. Budget for more than one sample round on any custom formula — the first round almost never ships unchanged.

How long does custom formulation take?

Trilogy Laboratories puts custom formulation at 3 to 9 months from concept to first production run, with production taking a further 6 to 8 weeks after development. Rhine Cosmetics gives a longer range for fully custom work — 9 to 18 months including research and development and 12 weeks of stability testing.

How long do reorders take?

Reorders of existing private-label products typically ship within 3 to 5 weeks once artwork and materials are on file (Trilogy Laboratories, checked 31 October 2026). That is roughly half the first-launch timeline, because sampling, artwork, and testing are already done — which is why reordering before you sell out matters.
