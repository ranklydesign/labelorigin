How to read it

Margin per unit is the money each sale keeps after paying for the product itself. Margin % is that figure as a share of the retail price, which is the number to compare across products and against the line you resell today. Monthly margin is margin per unit multiplied by units sold, minus the staff commission for the month — that is what the shelf actually earns you. Payback is how many units must sell before the month's commission is covered; every unit after that is margin.

Two things change the answer most. First, the gap between unit cost and retail price: a $2 change in either moves margin per unit by the same $2, and margin % moves with it. Second, the units sold: the same per-unit numbers look very different at 50 units a month and at 250, because commission is a fixed monthly amount spread across however many units sell. If payback is higher than the units you sell, the commission eats the whole month's margin — sell more units, raise the retail price, or renegotiate the commission before the line can earn anything.

If margin per unit is zero or negative, the retail price is at or below what you pay, and there is no payback to show. That is not a rounding problem; it means the price or the cost has to change before the line can earn anything. Frequently asked questions

What counts as unit cost?

What you pay the manufacturer for one finished, labeled unit. If your quote bundles fill, packaging, and labels into one per-unit figure, use that all-in figure. Keep it on the same basis as the retail price: both are per unit, so a per-unit cost against a per-set retail price will mislead you.

How do I enter staff commission?

Enter the total commission paid to staff on retail sales for the month, in dollars. If you pay commission per unit sold, multiply that per-unit figure by the units sold and enter the total. If you pay a percentage of the retail price, work out the dollar total for the month's units first — the calculator does not take a percentage, so the dollar figure keeps the math honest.

What margin % should a spa aim for?

There is no single published target, and this calculator does not set one. Compare the result against what you earn reselling someone else's line and against your treatment-room margins, and judge it against your own costs — rent, staff time, and the shelf space the line occupies. The calculator shows your number so the comparison is honest.

Is this a quote from a manufacturer?

No. Every figure comes from the numbers you enter. Nothing here is a price from any manufacturer. Use the result to judge real quotes: get every quote itemized on the same basis — same products, units, and packaging — or the totals will mislead you.
