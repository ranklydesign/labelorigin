Pre-tan prep: exfoliation before a spray tan

A spray tan is only as even as the skin underneath it. DHA reacts with the surface layer of skin, so prep decides the result. This guide covers the confirmed SASS-EE pre-tan lineup and the prep steps any salon can use.

Why prep matters

DHA reacts with the dead cells of the skin's surface layer, the stratum corneum. Uneven surface, uneven color: dry patches, leftover lotion and dead skin all take DHA differently, and the difference shows up as streaks and patchy fade.

Oils and heavy lotions can leave a film that interferes with the DHA reaction. That is generic pre-tan practice, not a claim about any one product — and it is the whole reason prep exists. Exfoliation clears the surface so the solution meets clean, even skin.

Barrier balm: the spots that pool

Barrier balm is used on hands, feet and nails — the places where color can pool and go dark. The catalog product is Cover Up Barrier Balm, in gallon or quart formats; the container and dose are not published, so ask for current specs before you plan how it is used at the station.

Barrier balm is back-bar, not retail. It belongs at the spray station, applied by the artist before the solution goes on.

The exfoliant you use in the appointment is the exfoliant you sell after it. Build prep into the service, and the retail bottle is the take-home version of what you just used.

A simple prep routine

The routine any salon can use, with no product claims attached:

  • Exfoliate before the appointment. Clear dead skin so the solution meets an even surface.
  • Arrive clean and dry. No heavy oils, lotions or deodorant on the skin at appointment time.
  • Barrier balm on the pooling spots. Hands, feet, nails — applied by the artist, before spraying.
  • Loose, dark clothing after. The color guide can transfer while it develops; clients should plan for it.

Your salon sets the exact timing. The point of the routine is consistency: the same prep every client, every time, so results stop varying by accident.
