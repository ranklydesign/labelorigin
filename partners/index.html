Who this is for

This page is for vendors whose clients are the same brands LabelOrigin talks to: spas, salons, retailers, and independent brands planning their own skincare or personal-care line. Four kinds of vendor fit most naturally, because each one answers a question a brand asks on the way to launch.

  • Packaging suppliers. Containers, closures, and outer packaging — the physical side of a line, where a brand's first questions are about what fits the product and what fits the shelf.
  • Label printers. Printed labels and the artwork-to-press handoff, where a brand needs to know what files to supply and how a label holds up on the container it chose.
  • Regulatory consultants. Help for a brand owner understanding labeling and regulatory duties that sit with them, and what evidence to keep. General guidance only — this is not legal advice, and neither side on this page provides it.
  • Business coaches. Coaches who work with spa, salon, and beauty founders on pricing, retail, and running the business behind the brand.

If you serve those brands in another way — photography, fulfillment, retail fixtures, or something adjacent — the same test applies: do your clients overlap with the audience reading our buyer guides? If they do, say so in your note and tell us how.

Three ways we co-promote

Co-promotion here is deliberately simple. It is not a sales channel, not an endorsement, and not a joint offer. It is three practical ways two businesses that serve the same buyers can be useful to each other, and to readers.

Link exchange

We link to each other where it genuinely helps the reader. A guide about choosing a manufacturer can point to a packaging supplier's range; a supplier's resources page can point to a guide that helps a brand owner ask better questions. Links go where they belong in the content, not in a footer list, and either side can ask for a link to come down.

Co-authored guides

Some topics need two kinds of knowledge. A guide on labels, for example, is stronger with a printer explaining file setup and print limits alongside a brand-owner view of what has to appear on the label. We decide the topic together, write in plain language, and publish on LabelOrigin's guides hub with credit to both sides. Each side can share and link to the finished guide.

Warm introductions

When a brand we are talking to needs what you do — packaging for a line that is ready to run, a printer for labels that are already designed — we introduce you by name, with context about what the brand is trying to do. And when a client of yours is weighing a private-label line, you can introduce them to LabelOrigin the same way. Introductions go one at a time, only when the fit is real, and never as a list handed over in bulk.

Co-marketing for consultants, schools, and software blogs

We also co-market with the people who teach and equip the same audience our guides serve: med spa consultants, esthetics schools, and spa-software blogs. If your readers or students are med spa owners, estheticians, or salon founders thinking about their own product line, the same three shapes apply — tuned to what you publish.

  • Guest guides. We write a guide for your audience — for example, how a med spa owner starts a private-label line, or what a new esthetician should know before retailing skincare — published on your blog or ours, with credit to both sides.
  • Co-branded checklists. A short, practical checklist we build together, such as a launch checklist for a spa's first skincare line or a retail-readiness checklist for graduates, carrying both names and free for either side to share.
  • Resource-link swaps. We link to each other's useful pages where it genuinely helps the reader — your business or training resources next to our buyer guides — no footer link lists, and either side can ask for a link to come down.

No referral fees and no money changes hands in any of these arrangements. If you are a med spa consultant, an esthetics school, or a spa-software blog, tell us which shape fits in the form below and we will reply with a next step or an honest no.

How it works

  1. Send a note. Use the form below. Tell us what you do, who you serve, and which of the three ways interests you — link exchange, a co-authored guide, or introductions. A link to your own site helps.
  2. We reply plainly. We review every note and reply with a next step or an honest no. If the audiences do not overlap, we say so rather than stretching a fit.
  3. Agree the shape. One arrangement at a time: what each side will do, where any link will sit, and what a co-authored guide will cover. Nothing is published or introduced before that is agreed.
  4. Do it, then review it. Links and guides go live, introductions happen one at a time. Either side can end the arrangement at any time, and links come down on request.

Co-promotion only. No money changes hands in these arrangements, and neither side promises the other any particular number of links, guides, or introductions. LabelOrigin does not manufacture anything — we build the funnels, guides, and outreach that bring buyers to manufacturers, and the manufacturer formulates and ships the products. A link or a shared guide is not an endorsement of any vendor, and a vendor's link to LabelOrigin is not an endorsement of LabelOrigin.

If you are a brand planning a line rather than a vendor, start with the buyer guides or the Renaud Naturals funnel, which asks for your business details and volume so the conversation begins from real numbers.
