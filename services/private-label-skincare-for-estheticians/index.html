What does a private-label line look like for an esthetician or day spa?

For an esthetician, a private-label line is usually two to four products: the cleanser and moisturizer you use in every facial, one treatment product clients ask about, and a body product. Clients buy what you use on them, so a short range is enough. A day spa can start wider, adding a product for the services that book most.

The economics are straightforward. You already recommend products after every appointment; today that recommendation sends clients to someone else's brand. Your own line keeps the retail margin in your studio and gives clients a reason to come back to you for refills instead of ordering online. The longer educational version of this page — small-batch volumes, the five steps, and what to ask a manufacturer — is the private-label guide for estheticians.

Objections, answered

What are the minimums?

Low minimums, sized to solo-practice and day-spa volumes — runs sized to months of real sales, so your cash stays in your business instead of on a shelf. We do not publish a number here because the per-SKU minimum depends on the formula and packaging you choose. Ask in the sample-request form and we will reply with the current per-SKU minimums for your shortlist, in writing, before you commit to anything. The trade-off is honest: per-unit costs run higher at small volumes — but a higher per-unit cost on product that sells beats a lower cost on product that expires.

How long does it take?

Stock formulas move faster than custom formulation, which needs development time and usually a second sample round. We do not quote a turnaround on this page because it depends on the route you choose and the formulas on your shortlist. Ask in the sample-request form and we will reply with the current timing for your line.

What about ingredients and compliance?

Ask for full ingredient lists before you approve anything, and ask what testing is included in the quote and what is billed separately: stability, preservative-effectiveness, and safety testing, itemized in the quote. Confirm who checks the label — labeling obligations generally sit with you as the brand owner. Also ask the sample question first: what does a sample round cost, and what is included in the per-unit cost — fill, packaging, and labels are often billed separately.

Who handles MoCRA?

MoCRA — the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 — expanded the FDA's authority over cosmetics, including facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation, adverse event reporting, and good manufacturing practice. The "responsible person" under MoCRA is the manufacturer, packer, or distributor whose name appears on the product label — for a private-label brand, that is usually you, the brand owner, not the contract manufacturer. The duties cannot be handed off by contract alone, but the practical work can be assigned: a manufacturer can register its facility, list products, and keep records. Confirm in writing who does what, and verify it. Our MoCRA guide for brand owners explains the duties in plain terms.

Start with what you already sell through. Look at your last three months: which products do clients rebuy, which do you recommend most, and which run out? Your first line is that list, branded — not a catalog of possibilities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Private label for estheticians
Can a solo esthetician really afford a private-label line?

Often yes, if the program is built for small batches — the whole point of low-minimum programs is runs sized to solo-practice volumes. The deciding numbers are the per-product minimum and the itemized per-unit cost, so get both in writing before committing.

How many products should my first line have?

Two to four. A cleanser and moisturizer you use daily, plus one treatment product clients ask about, is a complete launch. Every extra SKU multiplies minimums and shelf risk, so expand only when a product has earned its place through client demand.

What are the minimums?

Low minimums, sized to a practice rather than a national brand. The per-SKU minimum depends on the formula and packaging you choose, so we do not publish a number here — ask in the sample-request form and we will reply with the current per-SKU minimums for your shortlist in writing before you commit.

Who handles MoCRA compliance?

Under MoCRA, the responsible person is the manufacturer, packer, or distributor whose name appears on the product label — for a private-label brand, that is usually you, the brand owner. A manufacturer can do much of the practical work, such as registering its facility, listing products, and keeping records, but the legal duties stay with the brand owner. Confirm in writing who does what, and verify it.

Can I try the products before I commit?

Yes — request a sample kit and try the formulas in your treatment room, on skin, over days. Test the way your clients will use them, and treat a slow seller as information: drop it and double down on what moves.
