More than a third of beauty businesses publish no email address.

We checked 438 spa, med-spa, salon, hotel-spa, sunless, and indie beauty businesses while building outreach lists. 163 of them — 37.2% — publish no business email address anywhere a stranger can find one. The other 275 (62.8%) do. This page shows exactly how the count was done, the breakdown by segment, and what the gap means for brands and manufacturers trying to reach these businesses.

Compiled 21–28 October 2026 • 438 businesses • Public listings only • No lists purchased, no money spent gathering this data.

The headline finding

Of the 438 businesses tracked, 275 (62.8%) publish a business email address and 163 (37.2%) do not — under the counting rule described below. Every one of the 438 has a website; the gap is not about being offline. It is about whether a business that wants to be reached by suppliers, partners, and wholesale buyers publishes a direct email route for them.

The gap is uneven. Hotel and resort spas publish an email 73.3% of the time. Sunless tanning studios and massage/esthetics studios publish one less than half the time (46.3% and 48.1%). The segment a business sits in predicts its reachability better than its size does.

How the count was done

The 438 businesses come from LabelOrigin's own outreach tracking lists, built in five batches between 21 and 28 October 2026 from public listings: business websites, public directories, and search results. No purchased lists were used and no record was bought. Each business appears once — a name check found no duplicates and no business appears on both the email and no-email lists.

An address counted as a published business email only if the full address appeared on a public page — most often the business's own website, including contact and footer pages. In the final batch, only addresses directly visible on the business's own site counted. Earlier batches were re-checked in a second verification pass on 28 October 2026, and borderline cases were excluded: eight records carry notes explaining why an address was not counted, because it was redacted, garbled, or listed only by a third party rather than by the business itself. Every counted address was checked for a valid format.

A business landed on the no-email list when no address met that rule after checking its public pages — not when a single search came up empty. “No published email” therefore describes what a stranger can find, not what the business uses internally. A business may still be reachable through a contact form, direct messages, or phone.

What it means

For manufacturers and brands, the practical consequence is simple: email-first outreach cannot reach more than a third of this market. The other 163 businesses need a different first touch — a contact form, a direct message, or a phone call — each slower and harder to track than email. Any outreach plan built on email alone is, by construction, missing the sunless and massage segments most, where a majority of businesses publish no address.

For the businesses themselves, the gap cuts the other way. A spa or studio with no published email is harder for wholesale suppliers, private-label manufacturers, and partnership inquiries to reach. Those inquiries still arrive — through forms and messages — but they arrive mixed in with customer bookings and general questions, instead of at an address the owner checks for business opportunities. Publishing a business email does not guarantee more inquiries, but not publishing one guarantees that email inquiries go elsewhere: to a form, to a competitor, or nowhere.

Limitations — read these first

  • Not a census. This is an outreach-sourced sample of 438 businesses, not a random sample and not a census. Percentages describe this group only and must not be projected onto the whole industry.
  • Colorado-heavy start. The first batch of 106 businesses was Colorado-focused: 42 of the 106 (39.6%) are in Colorado. Later batches broadened coverage to all 50 states, but the sample still over-represents Colorado relative to the national market.
  • A snapshot in time. Pages were checked between 21 and 28 October 2026. Businesses add, remove, and mask email addresses regularly, so a business counted here as having no published email may publish one tomorrow, and vice versa.
  • “Not found” is not “does not exist.” Some addresses are masked from automated collection or sit behind forms. The counting rule measures public findability, not whether a business owns an inbox.
  • Segments are ours. The six groupings were assigned by LabelOrigin for outreach purposes. A business that spans segments — a day spa with a med-spa menu, for example — appears in one segment only, the one judged the best fit.
  • Three records lack a state. State coverage counts exclude three records with no state listed.

Frequently Asked Questions

The email gap study
Does “no published email” mean the business has no email at all?

No. It means no business email address was found under this study’s counting rule: a full address visible on a public page. The business may still be reachable through a contact form, direct messages, or phone, or it may mask its address from automated collection. “No published email” describes what a stranger can find, not what the business uses internally.

Is this a census of all spas, med spas, and salons?

No. This is a count of 438 businesses gathered for outreach between 21 and 28 October 2026, grouped into six segments defined by LabelOrigin. It is not a census and it is not a random sample, so the percentages describe this group only and should not be projected onto the whole industry.

Does this study name the businesses or share their contact details?

No. This page publishes aggregate counts only. No business is named and no email address is shared. Anyone doing outreach should use the contact route each business itself publishes, and respect opt-outs and do-not-contact requests.
